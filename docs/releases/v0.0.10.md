## Highlights ⭐️

* **A connector is who its certificate says**: the gateway now reads the SPIFFE ID from the connector's X.509-SVID and refuses a registration that claims another (`TW-R003`). Until now any trusted certificate could claim any identity. Breaking: connector certificates must be SVIDs; `tunnelward dev` issues one, and `tunnelward config check` catches a mismatch.
* **Routes have owners**: `[[connectors.owners]]` names who may claim a route, and a route nobody lists belongs to the identity holding it, so another identity cannot join its pool and take its traffic (`TW-R004`).
* **Limits that were only declared are enforced**: 60 registration attempts per source a minute (`TW-R009`), 1,024 domains per gateway (`TW-R010`) and 64 routes per identity (`TW-R005`), checked together so concurrent registrations cannot slip past them.
* **Release and CI hardening**: `cargo semver-checks` joins the gate, tests run on Linux aarch64, the CLA workflow's write permissions are scoped to its job, and crates.io publishing resumes after a partial run.
