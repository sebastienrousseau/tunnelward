Tunnelward is open core: the gateway is GPL-3.0-only, the SDK crates are
MIT OR Apache-2.0, and a commercial licence funds the work. Offering that
commercial licence for code someone else wrote needs their permission, so
every contribution comes with a CLA as well as the DCO sign-off.

- [Individual CLA](ICLA.md): you contribute on your own behalf.
- [Corporate CLA](CCLA.md): your employer owns what you write. A company
  signs once and lists the people allowed to contribute for it.

You keep the copyright in your contribution. The agreement gives the
project a licence to use it, including under other licences, and a patent
licence for it.

## How to sign

When you open a pull request, the CLA bot (`.github/workflows/cla.yml`)
asks you to comment once with the sentence it gives you. It records the
signature in the `cla-signatures` branch, and later pull requests pass
without asking again. For a corporate CLA, the company emails the signed
CCLA to the maintainer, who adds its contributors to the bot's allow list.
